# Clock- and reset-domain crossing checks

`mlint` checks every clock-domain crossing (CDC) and reset-domain crossing
(RDC) of the elaborated design. It needs no setup: the clocks and resets are
found from the RTL.

```sh
mlint rtl/*.sv -top soc_top                    # the findings
mlint rtl/*.sv -top soc_top -cdc-report        # plus every domain and crossing
mlint rtl/*.sv -top soc_top -clock-group clk,clk_div2 -static cfg_mode
mlint rtl/*.sv -top soc_top -enable RDC004 -Werror
```

## Rules

| Rule | What it finds | The usual fix |
|---|---|---|
| `CDC001` | A flop depends on a flop of another clock domain with no synchronizer, or through one flop only | Two flops in the reading domain |
| `CDC002` | A bus of more than one bit goes through a multi-flop synchronizer and is not Gray coded: its bits settle in different cycles | A handshake, an asynchronous FIFO, or a Gray code |
| `CDC003` | Combinational logic sits between the source flop and the synchronizer, so a glitch can be captured | Register the signal in its own domain first |
| `CDC004` | Reconvergence: two signals of one domain are synchronized separately and meet again, in the logic of one flop or up to `-cdc-depth` flops later, where they can be a cycle apart | Cross one signal and derive the rest from it |
| `CDC005` | One signal is synchronized more than once into the same domain; the copies can differ for a cycle | Synchronize once and share the result |
| `CDC006` | With `-sdc`: a flop's clock is not a clock in the SDC file | Add a `create_clock` or `create_generated_clock` for it |
| `RDC001` | An asynchronous reset is generated by a flop of another clock domain, so its release is not synchronous to the flops it resets | A reset synchronizer in the reading domain |
| `RDC002` | One asynchronous reset is used directly by flops of several clock domains | A reset synchronizer per domain |
| `RDC003` | In one clock domain, a flop with one asynchronous reset feeds a flop with another: when the first asserts alone, the second samples a changing value | Reset both together, or synchronize or gate the path |
| `RDC004` | The same, into a flop with no asynchronous reset. Off by default (`-enable RDC004`), because data-path flops without a reset are common | As RDC003 |

## What is a domain

A clock domain is the clock of an edge-triggered block, followed back through
port connections, buffers, inverters and clock gates (`clk & en_latched`) to
where it starts. A divided clock, a clock out of a mux, and each bit of a
clock bus (`clk[0]`, `clk[1]`) is a domain of its own.

In `always_ff @(posedge clk or negedge rst_n)` the edge the body tests
(`if (!rst_n)`) is the asynchronous reset and the other is the clock. A reset
that comes out of a reset synchronizer belongs to the reset that clears the
synchronizer, so `por_n` and its synchronized copies are one reset domain.

## What is recognized as safe

- **A synchronizer**: two or more flops in the reading domain, each a plain
  copy of the one before. Separate blocks, one block, with or without a reset,
  a shift register (`s <= {s[0], d}`), a loop over an array of stages, and a
  synchronizer cell instantiated per bit are all recognized.
- **An enable-qualified bus**: data crosses unsynchronized into a flop that
  also depends on a synchronized control from the same source domain. This is
  the mux recirculation scheme of a two-phase or four-phase handshake.
- **A Gray-coded bus** through a synchronizer: the source is `b ^ (b >> 1)`,
  written inline or in a binary-to-Gray module.

`-cdc-report` lists these too, so every crossing of the design is accounted
for:

```
clock domains: 2
  fifo.dst_clk_i  9 flops
  fifo.src_clk_i  6 flops
asynchronous resets: 2
  ...
crossings: 9
  fifo.i_src.wptr_q (fifo.src_clk_i) -> fifo.i_dst.gen_sync[0].i_sync.reg_q (fifo.dst_clk_i)  2-flop synchronizer, Gray coded
  fifo.i_src.data_q (fifo.src_clk_i) -> fifo.i_dst.data_q (fifo.dst_clk_i)  enable-qualified
```

## Constraints

- `-cdc-depth N`: how many flops of the reading domain `CDC004` follows a
  synchronized signal through to where it meets another (default 2; 0 looks
  at the logic of one flop only). A set of signals that meets after flops is
  reported once, at the nearest flop, with a count of the other flops it
  reaches.

- `-clock-group a,b[,c]`: these clocks are synchronous to each other (a clock
  and its divided clock, for example) and count as one domain. Repeatable.
- `-static sig`: the signal does not change while it is read (a configuration
  register written before the other domain starts), so its crossings are not
  reported. Repeatable.

Names are hierarchical paths, with or without the top (`top.u.clk` or
`u.clk`).

### From an SDC file

`-sdc file.sdc` (repeatable) reads the clocks and how they are related from
the timing constraints, with the SDC reader `msdc` and `msta` use, so the
crossing checks and timing agree on what a domain is:

```
mlint rtl/*.sv -top soc_top -sdc soc_top.sdc -cdc-report
```

| In the file | What it means for the crossing checks |
| --- | --- |
| `create_clock` on a port, pin or net | A clock starts there. A flop's domain is the clocks that reach its clock pin; behind a clock mux that is several |
| `create_generated_clock` | Synchronous to its master (`-master_clock`, or the clock that reaches `-source`), and in its master's group when a `set_clock_groups` does not list it |
| `set_clock_groups -asynchronous` | The groups are asynchronous to each other: paths between them are crossings. One `-group` alone is against every other clock |
| `set_clock_groups -logically_exclusive`, `-physically_exclusive` | The clocks are never there together (a functional and a test clock on one mux): paths between them are not checked |
| `set_false_path -from [get_clocks a] -to [get_clocks b]` | Timing no longer covers the path, so `a` and `b` are asynchronous and it needs a synchronizer |
| Any other pair of clocks | Synchronous, as for timing: no crossing |

A file that relates no clocks at all (no `set_clock_groups`, no false path
between clocks) would make every crossing vanish; mlint then takes clocks that
are not generated from one another as asynchronous, and says so.

`CDC006` reports a clock that drives flops but is not a clock in the file (a
divider with no `create_generated_clock`, most often). It is checked as a
domain of its own, asynchronous to every other.

Object names follow `msdc`: `/` between instance levels and no top name
(`u_a/u_b/clk`), a register `q` in `u_a` is the cell `u_a/q_reg` with pins
`CK`, `D` and `Q`, generate blocks are `name.` and `name[i].`. With `-sdc`,
`-cdc-report` prints the SDC clocks of every domain. `-clock-group` and
`-static` still apply on top of the file.

## Threads

`-threads N` sets the worker threads (default: every logical CPU; `-threads 1`
runs on one). The fan-in cone of every flop is traced and every crossing
classified in parallel, each flop its own task; the findings and the report
are the same whatever the count. The front end (parsing and elaboration) is
where most of the time goes and is outside this checker: on a generated
24,000-flop, 4-clock design the whole run is about 1.5 s, of which the CDC and
RDC analysis is 0.3 s.

## Limits

- Signals are tracked whole, not per bit, except where ports are connected to
  single bits of a bus. Two fields of one struct are one signal.
- Reconvergence is followed through `-cdc-depth` flops (2 by default), not
  further.
- Primary inputs have no domain: a crossing from an input is not reported.
- An enable that passes through another flop after its synchronizer and edge
  detect is not recognized as a qualifier.
- `MLINT_PROFILE=1` prints the time of each phase to stderr.

## Checked against

The clock-domain-crossing blocks of pulp-platform `common_cells` (two-phase
and four-phase handshakes, the Gray FIFO, the two-phase FIFO, the reset
controller, the edge propagator) with `tech_cells_generic` synchronizers come
out clean, with every crossing classified. The isochronous blocks, which rely
on related clocks, are reported until `-clock-group` says the clocks are
related. Ibex (one clock behind a clock gate) is one domain with no findings.
